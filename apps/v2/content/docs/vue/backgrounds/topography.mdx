## Animation

- The terrain is a field of gradient noise drawn as contour lines, like a topographic map: every fifth line is an index contour, thicker and brighter. The noise drifts slowly through time (`speed`), so the relief keeps reshaping itself.
- On mount, the relief rises from flat ground in 1.8s on an ease-out: the lines bloom out from the middle level.
- Under the cursor, a peak rises on a spring and follows it (`transition`), its rings tightening towards the summit (`peak`). Leaving the container lets it sink back.
- A click (or a tap) drops a wave: a crest and a trough travel out from the point in 2.6s, bunching the lines as they pass, and fade away.
- The lines take the container's text color, or `lineColor`. They're drawn with WebGL, pixel by pixel on the GPU (at most twice the device pixel ratio): no grid, so they glide without stepping and redraw on every frame, even at 120Hz and more. Where WebGL is missing, a canvas draws them as smooth curves instead.
- The loop pauses off screen and while the tab is hidden.
- Under reduced motion, the terrain is a still map: no drift, no peak, no wave.
